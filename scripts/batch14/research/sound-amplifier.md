# sound-amplifier research (WebSearch, 2026-10)
- FDA draws a line: hearing aids are medical devices for people with hearing loss; PSAPs (sound amplifiers) are electronic products intended for consumers with normal hearing.
- The FDA OTC hearing aid rule took effect October 17, 2022, letting adults with perceived mild to moderate loss buy certain hearing aids without a prescription or fitting.
- If hearing loss is suspected or diagnosed, an OTC or prescription hearing aid is the right category; a PSAP suits situational use (TV, quiet conversations).
- Situational TV listeners (for example TV Ears style systems) let one person raise volume and tone without disturbing others.
- Rule out earwax or infection first; an audiologist check is recommended before buying a device.
- PSAPs and OTC aids are usually not covered by insurance or Medicare.
- One listing cites a SuperEar SE7500 at up to 80 hours on two AAA batteries (a manufacturer claim); battery life varies by model.
- Feedback (whistling) and directional-mic behavior are rarely documented independently; check reviews for these.
