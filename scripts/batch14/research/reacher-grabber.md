# reacher-grabber research (WebSearch, 2026-10)
- Length: about 32 inches suits general use; 48 to 52 inch models add leverage weight and need more strength to control.
- Weight: for frail users one source recommends under 1.2 lb to reduce shoulder strain.
- Jaw opening: about 3 inches handles most items; 3.5 inches suits larger items. Rubberized or silicone tips grip smooth objects (plates, bottles) where bare plastic slips.
- Trigger effort is the main issue for weak hands; a whole-hand or pistol-style grip is easier than a one-finger trigger for arthritis.
- Rated lift varies widely (one listing 5 lb, a heavy-duty outdoor model 20 lb). No general safe standard exists; use the manufacturer rating and test with a full coffee mug.
- Magnetic tips help with keys and dropped metal items; folding models store in a car or walker bag.
- Technique: keep a wide stance, face the item, use your free arm as a third point of support, and never turn sideways to reach overhead (home health therapist guidance).
- A reacher can raise fall risk without instruction, especially with hip or bending restrictions; keep daily items within easy reach.
- Seller claims on grip are not independent tests; try before relying on one for stairs or ladders.
