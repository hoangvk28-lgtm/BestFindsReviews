# portable-charger research (WebSearch, 2026-10)
- Compare in watt-hours, not mAh: Wh = mAh / 1000 x voltage (usually 3.6 to 3.7 V). The voltage assumption matters near limits: 27,000 mAh is about 99.9 Wh at 3.7 V but about 97 Wh at 3.6 V.
- Airline tiers cited: 100 Wh or less allowed in cabin without approval; 100 to 160 Wh needs airline approval (typically max two); above 160 Wh not allowed. Carry in cabin bags and check your airline.
- Examples: 20,000 mAh is about 74 Wh; 26,800 mAh about 99 Wh; 30,000 mAh about 108 to 111 Wh (needs approval). Look for a printed Wh rating or security may confiscate it.
- Usable output is less than rated: independent tests show roughly 77 to 83% (a 36 Wh Anker delivered about 30 Wh); blogs claim 60 to 80%.
- Speed depends on watts: USB-C PD 3.0 spans 18 to 100 W versus 5 to 12 W on typical USB-A ports. Short quality cables lose less.
- Look for UL or FCC marks and built-in protection against overcharge and short circuit.
- Two 20,000 mAh banks can beat one 40,000 mAh (backup, sharing, ports), per one retailer.
