# wireless-charger research (WebSearch, 2026-10)
- Real speed depends on phone, coil alignment, and case thickness; iPhones cap at 7.5 W on generic Qi pads unless the charger uses MagSafe-style magnets.
- Qi2 adds magnetic alignment, improving efficiency and cutting heat; phones without built-in magnets need a magnetic case.
- Cases: roughly 3 mm or thinner works through most chargers; thick or metal cases cut efficiency or block charging.
- A 15 W charger needs about a 20 W USB-C PD wall adapter for full output; the bundled cable alone is not enough.
- Foreign object detection shuts power off when metal (keys, coins) is on the pad; look for overheat protection too.
- Marketing claims of 15 W or 25 W are often misaligned-coil best cases; sources contradict each other on 25 W phones, so verify with the phone maker or the Wireless Power Consortium listing.
- Helpful for older users: no tiny charging port to line up; a magnetic or raised cradle shows the phone is positioned correctly.
- Pacemaker/implant guidance: sources did not address it; check the device maker's guidance and your cardiologist.
