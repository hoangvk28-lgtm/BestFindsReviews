# smart-bulb research (WebSearch, 2026-10)
- A bulb needs constant power: a wall switch, pull chain, or breaker cut kills the radio, so apps and voice commands fail. Leave the switch on, or use a smart switch, or fit a switch cover.
- Wi-Fi bulbs usually need a 2.4 GHz network; joining 5 GHz is a common setup failure. Hub-based Zigbee or Thread feels more reliable beyond about 10 to 15 lights.
- Thread needs a border router; Matter needs both bulb and controller to be Matter-certified. Matter reduces lock-in but does not fix a bad bulb.
- After a power outage, bulbs can return at full brightness (Hue defaults to 100% warm white) or become unlinked from a hub; check power-on behavior per bulb.
- CRI: 90+ is excellent, low 80s acceptable; one test found a budget RGB bulb under 40 CRI at its warmest setting. RGBWW or RGBTW bulbs give cleaner whites than RGB-only.
- Brightness: about 800 lumens for a standard A19; 1,100 to 1,600 lumens for brighter models.
- Tunable white range of roughly 2200 to 2700 K for evenings and 6500 K for tasks.
- Voice-command failures usually mean connectivity problems, not a faulty assistant.
