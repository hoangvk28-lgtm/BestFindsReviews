# reading-lamp research (WebSearch, 2026-10)
- The AARP/Rensselaer guide says older eyes need at least three times more light for fine detail and suggests at least 1,000 lux (100 fc) on the task; one low-vision lamp lists 3,500 lux at 30 cm (12 in) as a product spec.
- Placement: put the task lamp above and behind the reader, aimed at the page, with an opaque shade so the bright source is not visible.
- Glare sensitivity increases with age: avoid bare bulbs and shiny louvers; more light helps only if glare is controlled.
- The American Lighting Association suggests 2700 to 3000 K and CRI 80 or higher; seniors guides prefer a lamp that spans 2700 to 5000 K and CRI 90+ for natural-looking print and photos.
- Dimmability matters: three-way or stepless dimming lets people match brightness to contrast and fatigue.
- Low-vision-oriented lamps (RNIB-linked) add selectable color temperatures, low-heat shades, and an adjustable arm.
- Stability: weighted base and a tip-resistant arm at full extension; route cords away from walking paths.
- Controls: large touch panel or inline switch beats tiny sensors for arthritic fingers.
- No authoritative clinical standard for a "senior reading lamp" exists; individual vision varies, so check with a low-vision specialist.
