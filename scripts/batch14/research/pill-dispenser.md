# pill-dispenser research (WebSearch, 2026-10)
- Most reviews cluster around 28-day or 31-day capacity, sound plus light alarms, and some lock to prevent double dosing.
- A good dispenser keeps missed pills in the tray and locks until the next alarm; alarm windows vary by model (one clinical guide cites 1 or 5 hours).
- Remote caregiver alerts (text, push, call) usually need Wi-Fi and a stable home network; simple beep-and-flash units work without Wi-Fi but caregivers must check in person.
- App-dependent features (fingerprint lock, push alerts) lose about half their value if the user has no smartphone. Wi-Fi pairing is a recurring complaint.
- Ask what happens in a power outage: battery backup decides whether a refill stays usable. Some units have no rechargeable battery and need cell swaps about every 60 days; USB cords can get knocked loose.
- Lock types: key lock, PIN, fingerprint. Mechanical key override lets caregivers in during emergencies.
- Check how easy loading is for arthritic hands; dexterity matters as much as software.
- Dispensers do not replace supervision for advanced dementia; ask the prescriber or pharmacist which device fits the regimen.
- Caution: many "cuts errors by half" statistics on affiliate sites are unverified; do not repeat them.
