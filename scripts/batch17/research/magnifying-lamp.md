# Research: magnifying lamp (ease of use, not medical)

- Diopter measures lens power; approximate magnification = diopter / 4 + 1. So 3 diopter is about 1.75X, 5 diopter about 2.25X, 12 diopter about 4X, 20 diopter about 6X. Listings quoting 10X, 12X, 15X, 30X use a different (hand-lens style) convention and imply a much closer working distance.
- Higher power means a shorter working distance and a smaller field of view. At 10X to 15X you often work within 2 to 4 inches of the lens, which is awkward for reading and sewing.
- Larger lens shows more of the page or fabric at once; small high-power lenses suit fine detail only.
- Choose the lowest power that shows the detail you need, then a lens size that keeps enough of the work in view.
- Daylight-style light is commonly around 5000 to 6500K; lamps often offer adjustable color temperature and dimming. Warm light is gentler for long sessions; cool light gives crisp contrast. Stepless dimming helps with glare on glossy paper.
- CRI (color rendering) around 90+ helps color matching for sewing, painting and crafts when listed.
- Clamp mounts suit a fixed table with an edge of suitable thickness (clamp openings vary from about 1 inch to 2.5 inches). Weighted bases suit glass tabletops, frequent moves and surfaces that cannot take a clamp; floor stands free the desk but need floor space.
- Longer arms reach across a bench but add leverage: a loose clamp or light base can wobble or droop; check arm stiffness.
- Glass lenses are generally sharper and scratch resistant but heavier and more breakable; acrylic is lighter and tougher against drops but can scratch if wiped dry with a dusty cloth.
- Lens care: dust with a soft brush or blower first, then a clean microfiber cloth; use the dust cover when provided; avoid abrasive or ammonia cleaners on coatings.
- Controls on the lamp head or large buttons are easier than hunting for a rear switch; memory of last setting avoids resetting.
- Rechargeable models trade a cord for charge cycles; run time varies and battery capacity (mAh) is a rough guide.
