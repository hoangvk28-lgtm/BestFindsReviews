# Research: flip phone (ease of use, not medical)

- The major US carriers retired their 3G networks in 2022, so a flip phone must use 4G LTE with VoLTE (voice over LTE) to place ordinary calls. A phone that powers on but cannot call is usually missing VoLTE support or provisioning for the specific carrier.
- AT&T, T-Mobile and Verizon use different LTE bands and often whitelist devices separately. A phone tuned to one carrier's bands can give weak or no service on another, so "unlocked" does not mean "works everywhere".
- Many budget unlocked flip phones are tuned mainly for T-Mobile and the MVNOs that use its network. Verizon and AT&T often need a model they have certified; confirm with the carrier's device-compatibility page using the phone's IMEI or model number.
- Carrier-locked phones (for example AT&T or Verizon prepaid models) only work on that carrier; prepaid-locked units may not activate on postpaid plans.
- Lively Jitterbug phones are sold to work only with Lively service, with a pre-installed SIM and an activated plan required.
- A SIM locked to a fixed-rate plan or a subsidized program may not work in third-party unlocked phones. Fresh SIMs sometimes need activating in another device first.
- Hearing aid compatibility: phones rated M3 or M4 (microphone / acoustic coupling) and T3 or T4 (telecoil / inductive coupling) meet FCC requirements, with 4 the higher rating. The hearing aid has its own M/T immunity rating; adding the aid's rating to the phone's rating, a sum of 6 or more suggests excellent performance. Not every hearing aid has a telecoil. A rating shown on the listing is the maker's claim; check the box or the carrier's HAC page.
- KaiOS is a lightweight OS used on some flip phones with a small app store (maps, browser, YouTube, voice assistant in some listings). App availability, including messaging apps such as WhatsApp, has changed over time and is not guaranteed; verify with the maker before buying.
- Android-based flips (Go edition) can use more apps but are heavier, drain faster and are more complex.
- Charging docks (cradles) let the phone drop in without aligning a small cable port, which helps people with limited dexterity; check whether the dock is included and whether a cable is also supplied.
- Removable batteries allow swapping a spare; battery capacity (mAh) is a rough guide, and listed talk time is a maker figure.
- Dual screens: an external screen shows caller, time and notifications without opening the phone; flip-to-answer and close-to-hang-up reduce button presses.
- SOS buttons on basic phones are typically a calling feature that dials preset contacts in turn and sends a text; it is not the same as a monitored emergency response service. Set it up and test with family before relying on it.
- Rugged flips (IP68, MIL-STD-810) trade pocket thinness and weight for water and drop resistance; ports and battery covers must be closed to keep the rating.
- Renewed (refurbished) listings vary in condition and warranty; new units usually carry a maker warranty.
- Physical keys: larger, raised, backlit keys with distinct call and end keys reduce misdials; speed dial slots (2-9, M1/M2) give one-press calling.
- Camera quality on basic flips is modest (typically 0.3 to 5 megapixels); a smartphone-class flip has far better imaging but a much steeper learning curve.
