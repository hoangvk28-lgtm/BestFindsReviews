# Research: bed wedge pillow (comfort and positioning framing, no medical claims)

- Right height depends on the activity: reading/working wants a steeper incline, TV/relaxing medium, gentle sleep elevation lower.
- Fixed-height wedge suits one job; adjustable (multi-piece, headrest) suits sitting up, reclining and sleeping raised.
- Common retailer ranges: about 6-8 in for gentle elevation, 7-12 in for people who want more upright sleep; 10-12 in is the taller, near-upright option.
- Height and angle are linked: roughly 30 degrees ~ 7-8 in high, 45 degrees ~ 10-12 in high. Manufacturers vary, so read the listed height.
- Size vs bed and body: wedge length and width should match your torso and mattress; too short and shoulders hang off.
- Foam: CertiPUR-US is a commonly cited foam standard; high-density base for durability, memory foam top layer (about 1-1.5 in) for comfort.
- Removable, washable cover helps with allergies and upkeep; non-slip base reduces sliding.
- Sliding down the wedge is a frequent complaint: smooth covers, soft slick fabrics and low bed-friction surfaces make it worse; straps/non-slip backing help.
- Headboard gap fillers are rated by gap range (e.g. 0 to 5.5 or 0 to 6 in). Measure the gap first. Too small a filler slips through.
- Thick mattresses or high frames widen the gap; memory foam toppers can drift away from the headboard.
- Filler materials: memory foam or polyurethane foam; inflatable options exist for irregular gaps. Rolled towels are a stopgap.
- A wedge is a comfort/positioning product, not a treatment for snoring, apnea or reflux; persistent symptoms belong with a doctor.
- Hygiene: gaps collect dust; a filler closes them off.
