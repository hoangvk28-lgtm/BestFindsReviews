# Research: smart alarm clock

- Sunrise alarms ramp light over a set window (commonly 5-60 min in this lineup); longer ramps feel gentler, and a sound backup matters for deep sleepers.
- Guides for heavy sleepers cite alarm volume of 85 dB or more as a practical floor; many dedicated loud clocks advertise 110-115 dB.
- Vibrating bed shakers add a physical cue; they matter most for deep sleepers and users with hearing loss.
- Light-based wake-ups work best in a darkened room with the lamp facing the sleeper; a blocked or distant lamp weakens the effect.
- Brightness figures (lux) are rarely published on budget clocks; judge by ramp length and dimmer range instead.
- Plug-in clocks without battery backup lose the alarm in an outage; a coin-cell or AAA backup preserves settings.
- Auto time set (SmartSet style) and auto daylight saving cut the most common failure: a wrong alarm time after a power cut or clock change.
- Large digits (about 2 inches or more) and a full-range dimmer help older eyes; bright displays disturb sleep.
- App-controlled clocks depend on 2.4GHz WiFi and the phone app; if the app is discontinued, features can vanish.
- Some brand models bundle a paid subscription tier for extra content; always confirm which features are included at purchase.
- Sound machines help mask noise but add menu complexity; physical buttons stay easier to operate half-asleep.
- Talking clocks announce time and date and can offer medication reminders, helpful for low vision.
- Snooze length (commonly 9 minutes) and alarm duration settings change how forgiving a clock is for heavy sleepers.
- Wireless charging pads on clock radios need a compatible phone and a case thin enough to charge through.
- Matching an alarm to a consistent schedule (dual alarm weekday/weekend) reduces missed wake-ups.
