# Research: smart plug

- Most North American smart plugs are rated 15A / 1800W; check the rating against the appliance, and avoid heat-producing loads such as space heaters unless the rating clearly covers them.
- Look for UL or ETL certification on the housing, plus overload protection.
- Nearly all Wi-Fi plugs connect to 2.4GHz only; a phone on 5GHz can fail during setup until switched or the router splits bands.
- Matter-certified plugs work across Apple Home, Alexa, Google Home and SmartThings and can keep local control when the internet is down.
- Energy data often does not pass through Matter, so a separate brand app may be needed to see consumption.
- Alexa-only plugs are simplest for an Echo household but lock you into one ecosystem.
- Third-party plugs from white-label brands often route through the Smart Life or Tuya app before linking to voice assistants.
- A compact body that leaves the second outlet free matters on a wall duplex; bulky plugs block it.
- Smart plugs only help for devices with a physical on switch left in the on position; devices needing a button press after power returns will not restart.
- Schedules, countdown timers and sunrise/sunset triggers handle lamps, fans and holiday lights without voice.
- Multi-packs lower the cost per plug but only help if you will actually use several.
- Outdoor plugs need a weather-resistant cover and an outdoor rating.
- Power strips with individual smart outlets suit a desk or entertainment center with several devices.
- Plugs that need a cloud account stop working remotely if the maker shuts the service down.
