# Research: rocker knife (seed)

- A rocker knife has a curved blade pressed down and rocked forward; the force comes from the shoulder and forearm rather than the wrist.
- T-shaped or vertical handles keep the hand above the blade and turn downward pressure into cutting force.
- Rocking, not sawing, is the intended motion; the edge is often rounded at the corners.
- Non-slip handle material helps when hands are damp or grip is limited.
- Wood handles are usually dishwasher limited to lower temperatures than plastic handles; check each listing.
- A snap-on or slide-on blade cover protects the edge in drawers.
- A weighted handle or hand cuff adds stability for people who cannot close the hand.
- Single-edged blades may be sharpened with a standard sharpener on some models.
- Rocker knives work best on a stable board; a non-slip mat or one-handed spiked board holds food in place.
- Curved ulu-style blades double as mezzalunas for herbs and pizza.
- An occupational therapist can match handle shape and size to a specific hand.
